2.1 Properties of Real Numbers

The properties of real numbers are fundamental rules that allow expressions to be rearranged, simplified, and solved without changing their value.

🔄 Commutative Property

The order of numbers does not change the result in addition or multiplication.

\[ a + b = b + a \quad \quad ab = ba \]
Example: \(4 + 7 = 7 + 4\) \[ 11 = 11 \]
💡 Tip

If you are only adding or multiplying, you can change the order freely.

🩺 Health Application

In dosage calculations or medical measurements, the order of addition does not affect the final result.

🔗 Associative Property

Grouping numbers does not change the result in addition or multiplication.

\[ (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) \]
Example: \((2+3)+5 = 2+(3+5)\) \[ 10 = 10 \]
💡 Tip

Group numbers that are easier to compute mentally.

🩺 Health Application

In medical data processing, grouping calculations with the associative property can facilitate faster analysis.

📦 Distributive Property

Allows multiplying a number by each term inside parentheses.

\[ a(b + c) = ab + ac \]
Example: \(3(x+2)\) \[ 3x + 6 \]
💡 Tip

Remember: the number outside multiplies everything inside the parentheses.

🩺 Health Application

The distributive property is used in radiology models to calculate costs or doses distributed across factors.

➖ Properties of Negative Numbers

Negative numbers follow specific rules in addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Understanding these rules is essential to avoid algebraic mistakes.

  • Negative × Negative = Positive
  • Positive × Negative = Negative
  • Negative × Positive = Negative
  • Negative ÷ Negative = Positive
  • Positive ÷ Negative = Negative
  • The sum of two negative numbers is always negative.
  • Subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding.
Examples:
  • \((-2)(-3)= 6\). Because Negative × Negative equals Positive.
  • \((-12) \div (-4)= 3\). Because Negative ÷ Negative Negative equals Positive.
  • \(-5 + (-2)=-5 -2= -7\). Because Positive × Negative equals Negative and the sum of two negative numbers is always negative.
  • \(7 - (-3)= 7 + 3 = 10\). Because Negative × Negative equals Positive.
💡 Tip

Two equal signs produce a positive result. Two different signs produce a negative result.

⚠ Common Mistake

Many students forget that subtracting a negative changes the operation into addition.

🩺 Health Application

These properties are important for interpreting direction changes, intensity differences, and value variations in medical imaging and radiologic measurements.

2.2 Absolute Value

Absolute value represents the distance from a number to zero on the number line. Since distance can never be negative, absolute value is always positive or zero.

\[ |a| \geq 0 \]
Example: Find the absolute value of \(-7\). \[ |-7| = 7 \]

Even though \(-7\) is negative, its distance from zero is \(7\).

🧠 Concept

Absolute value removes the negative sign and keeps only the distance from zero.

Properties of Absolute Value

1. Product Property

The absolute value of a product is equal to the product of the absolute values.

\[ |ab| = |a||b| \]
Example: \[ |(-3)(4)| = |-12| = 12 \] \[ |{-3}||4| = (3)(4) = 12 \]
💡 Tip

You can multiply first and then take the absolute value, or take the absolute value of each number before multiplying. The result will be the same.

⚠ Common Mistake

Do not forget that absolute value is always positive.

🩺 Health Application

In medical imaging, some formulas multiply positive and negative intensity changes. Absolute value allows working with magnitudes regardless of direction.

2. Quotient Property

The absolute value of a quotient is equal to the quotient of the absolute values.

\[ \left|\frac{a}{b}\right| = \frac{|a|}{|b|} \]
Example: \[ \left|\frac{-18}{3}\right| = |-6| = 6 \] \[ \frac{|-18|}{|3|} = \frac{18}{3} = 6 \]
💡 Tip

Absolute value can be applied before or after dividing.

⚠ Common Mistake

Never leave a negative result inside the final absolute value.

🩺 Health Application

In calculations related to medical signals or measurement variations, absolute value helps represent real differences regardless of sign.

Why Are These Properties Important?

  • They simplify algebraic expressions involving negative numbers.
  • They allow correct work with distances and magnitudes.
  • They are used in algebra, physics, statistics, and medical imaging.
  • They help avoid sign errors in mathematical calculations.

2.3 Distance

The distance between two real numbers represents how far apart they are on the number line. In mathematics, distance is always expressed as a positive value or zero because a distance cannot be negative.

The distance formula between two real numbers uses absolute value:

\[ d(a,b)=|a-b| \]
🧠 Concept

Absolute value removes negative signs and guarantees that distance is always positive.

Example: Find the distance between \(3\) and \(-2\). \[\begin{array}{rcl} d(3,-2) & = & |3-(-2)|\\ & = & |3+2| \\ & = & |5|\\ & = & 5 \end{array} \]

Properties of Distance

1. Every distance is positive.

Distance represents separation between points. Since separation can never be negative, the result will always be positive or zero.

Example: Find the distance between \(-4\) and \(2\). \[\begin{array}{rcl} d(-4,2) & = & |-4-2|\\ & = & |-6| \\ & = & 6 \end{array} \]
💡 Tip

Remember: distance measures separation, not direction. That is why it can never be negative.

🩺 Health Application

In radiological imaging, the distance between the X-ray tube and the patient affects image quality. Positional differences are always measured as positive quantities.

2. The distance between two identical points is zero.

If two numbers occupy exactly the same position on the number line, there is no separation between them.

Example: Find the distance between \(5\) and \(5\). \[\begin{array}{rcl} d(5,5) & = & |5-5| \\ & = & |0| \\ & = & 0 \end{array} \]
💡 Tip

If both numbers are equal, the distance will automatically be zero.

⚠ Common Mistake

Do not confuse distance with individual absolute value. Distance depends on comparing two numbers.

🩺 Health Application

In radiology, image brightness may depend on the distance between the X-ray tube and the patient.

If the optimal distance is \(100\) cm and the tube was placed at \(97\) cm, the difference can be calculated using absolute value.

\[|97-100|=3\]

The actual difference is \(3\) cm regardless of the direction of the error.

2.4 Application of Inequalities and Distance in Radiological Results

In radiological studies and diagnostic testing, mathematics helps compare values, identify normal ranges, and calculate differences between measurements. Concepts such as inequalities, absolute value, and distance are frequently used to interpret clinical results.

🩺 Clinical Report

The following table presents simplified results from a radiological study with examples of comparison, ordering, and numerical distance.

Study Results

Parameter Result Mathematical Interpretation
Detector Temperature (\(^\circ C\)) \(-3^\circ C\) Negative number less than zero
Tube-to-patient distance \(97 \text{ cm}\) Compared with ideal value of \(100\text{ cm}\)
Contrast Index \(0.92\) Positive rational number
Minimum acceptable level \(\geq 0.85\) Use of inequality
Difference between expected and actual measurement \(|97-100|=3\) Absolute value and distance

Mathematical Interpretation

Comparing Numbers: The detector temperature is \(-3^\circ C\), meaning it is below \(0^\circ C\). Mathematically, this is written as:

\[ -3 < 0 \]

This represents an inequality between real numbers.

Using Inequalities: The minimum acceptable index for clear images is \(0.85\). The study obtained \(0.92\).

\[ 0.92 > 0.85 \]

Since the obtained value is greater than the required minimum, the image quality is acceptable.

Absolute Value and Distance: The ideal distance between the tube and the patient is \(100\text{ cm}\), but the actual distance was \(97\text{ cm}\).

\[ |97-100|=3 \]

Absolute value shows that there is a difference of \(3\text{ cm}\), regardless of whether the value is above or below the ideal measurement.

🧠 Mathematical Connection

Inequalities help verify whether a value is within a safe or acceptable range, while absolute value measures actual differences between expected and obtained values.

💡 Insight

In health sciences, small numerical differences may affect the diagnostic quality of a medical image.

⚠ Common Mistake

Do not confuse absolute value with simply removing negative signs. Absolute value represents real distance or difference.
