3. Powers and Algebraic Expressions

This chapter introduces powers, exponent rules, and algebraic expressions used in mathematics and health science applications.

Introductory Example: Simplify \(x^3 \cdot x^2\). \[ x^3 \cdot x^2 = x^5 \]

3.1 Powers

A power represents repeated multiplication of the same number.

\[ a^n \]

In the previous expression, \(a\) is the base and \(n\) is the exponent.

Example: Calculate \(2^4\). \[ 2^4 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 16 \]

3.2 Order of Operations

When an expression contains several operations, they must be solved following a specific order.

  1. Parentheses
  2. Powers
  3. Multiplication and Division
  4. Addition and Subtraction
Example: Solve \(3 + 2^2 \cdot 5\). \[ 3 + 2^2 \cdot 5 \] \[ = 3 + 4 \cdot 5 \] \[ = 3 + 20 \] \[ = 23 \]

3.3 Powers with Zero Exponent

Any nonzero base raised to the zero exponent equals one.

\[ a^0 = 1 \]
Example: Simplify \(5^0\). \[ 5^0 = 1 \]

3.4 Powers with Negative Exponents

A negative exponent represents the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power.

\[ a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \]
Example: Simplify \(2^{-3}\). \[ 2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8} \]

3.5 Rules of Exponents

Rules of exponents allow algebraic expressions to be simplified.

Product of Powers

\[ a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n} \]

Quotient of Powers

\[ \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \]

Power of a Power

\[ (a^m)^n = a^{mn} \]
Example: Simplify \(x^2 \cdot x^5\). \[ x^2 \cdot x^5 = x^7 \]

3.6 Variables, Coefficients, and Constants

A variable is a symbol representing an unknown or changing number.

A coefficient is the number that accompanies a variable.

A constant is a fixed value that does not change.

Example: In \(5x + 3\), the coefficient is \(5\), the variable is \(x\), and the constant is \(3\).

3.7 Algebraic Expressions

An algebraic expression is a combination of variables, numbers, and mathematical operations.

Example: \(3x^2 + 2x - 5\) is an algebraic expression.

Algebraic Terms

Each term of an algebraic expression is separated by addition or subtraction signs.

3.8 Applications in Radiology

Algebraic expressions and powers are used in formulas related to radiation and image quality.

Applied Example: Radiation intensity decreases as distance increases. \[ I = \frac{k}{d^2} \]
Applied Example: Image quality depends on time and electric current. \[ mAs = mA \times s \]

3.9 Application of Powers and Algebraic Expressions in Radiological Studies

In radiology and health sciences, mathematics helps analyze values related to radiation intensity, image resolution, exposure, and digital processing. Powers and algebraic expressions help represent relationships between variables and simplify calculations used in diagnostic equipment.

🩺 Radiological Report

The following table presents simplified results from a radiological study where powers and algebraic expressions are used.

Study Results

Parameter Result Mathematical Interpretation
Radiation Intensity \(2^4\) Use of exponents
Amplification Level \(5^2\) Positive exponent
Algebraic Model \(3x+2\) Algebraic expression
Reduction Factor \(10^{-2}\) Negative exponent
Digital Detector Area \(8^2\) Exponent applied to measurements

Mathematical Interpretation

Using Powers: Radiation intensity was represented using \(2^4\).

\[ 2^4 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \] \[ =16 \]

This means the intensity increased by multiplying the same base value four times.

Negative Exponents: Some equipment uses reduction factors expressed with negative exponents.

\[ 10^{-2}=\frac{1}{10^2} \] \[ =\frac{1}{100} \] \[ =0.01 \]

Negative exponents represent small values or reduction processes.

Algebraic Expressions: The model \(3x+2\) may represent the relationship between exposure time and intensity level.

If \(x=4\):

\[ 3(4)+2 \] \[ =12+2 \] \[ =14 \]

Evaluating algebraic expressions allows obtaining specific results from variables.

Area and Powers: The digital detector has an area of \(8^2\).

\[ 8^2=64 \]

Powers are frequently used to calculate areas and dimensions in medical imaging.

🧠 Mathematical Connection

Powers simplify repetitive calculations, while algebraic expressions represent relationships between variables in clinical and technological contexts.

💡 Insight

In digital radiology, small variations in intensity or exposure may produce major changes in diagnostic image quality.

⚠ Common Mistake

Do not confuse multiplication with exponents. In \(2^4\), the number \(2\) multiplies itself four times, not by \(4\).

🩺 Clinical Importance

Medical imaging equipment uses mathematical models to control exposure, resolution, and digital processing. Understanding powers and algebraic expressions helps interpret how these systems operate.
