3.1 Powers

A power is a shorthand way to represent repeated multiplication of the same number or expression.

Power Notation

\[ a^n \]

The base is \(a\), and the exponent is \(n\). The exponent indicates how many times the base is used as a factor.

\[ a^n=\underbrace{a\cdot a\cdot a\cdots a}_{n\text{ factors}} \]

Interpreting a Power

To calculate a power with a positive exponent, multiply the base by itself as many times as indicated by the exponent.

\[ 2^4=2\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=16 \]

The base is \(2\) and the exponent is \(4\).

Basic Powers

\[ \begin{array}{c|cccc} \text{Base} & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5\\ \hline \text{Cuadrado} & 4 & 9 & 16 & 25\\ \text{Cubo} & 8 & 27 & 64 & 125 \end{array} \]

Negative Bases

When the base is negative, parentheses clearly identify which number is being raised to a power.

\[ (-2)^4=16 \qquad (-2)^3=-8 \]

With an even exponent, the result is positive; with an odd exponent, the result is negative.

Watch the Signs

\[ (-2)^4=16 \qquad\text{but}\qquad -2^4=-16 \]

Parentheses determine whether the negative sign is part of the base.

Powers of Fractions

\[ \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{2^2}{3^2} = \frac{4}{9} \]

3.2 Order of Operations

When an expression contains several operations, a specific order must be followed to obtain one correct result.

Order of Operations

  1. Parentheses and other grouping symbols.
  2. Powers.
  3. Multiplication and division, from left to right.
  4. Addition and subtraction, from left to right.

Powers Within an Expression

Powers are calculated before multiplication, division, addition, or subtraction, unless grouping symbols indicate a different order.

\[ 3+2^2(5) \] \[ =3+4(5) \] \[ =3+20=23 \]

Operations Within the Base

If the base of a power contains an operation, first evaluate the expression inside the parentheses and then apply the exponent.

\[ (2+3)^2 = 5^2 = 25 \]

This is different from \(2+3^2=11\).

3.3 Powers with Zero Exponent

Any power with a nonzero base and a zero exponent equals one.

\[ \boxed{a^0=1\qquad\text{if }a\neq0} \]

Applying the Rule

\[ 7^0=1 \qquad (-4)^0=1 \qquad \left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^0=1 \]

In Algebraic Expressions

The same rule applies when the base is a variable or a nonzero algebraic expression.

\[ x^0=1 \qquad (3x)^0=1 \qquad (x+5)^0=1 \]

Negative Sign and Zero Exponent

\[ (-5)^0=1 \qquad\text{while}\qquad -5^0=-1 \]

Parentheses determine the base of the power.

Application in Health Sciences

In mathematical models, a variable or expression raised to zero can represent a multiplicative factor equal to one, provided that the base is nonzero.

3.4 Powers with Negative Exponents

A negative exponent indicates that the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power must be used.

\[ \boxed{a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}\qquad\text{if }a\neq0} \]

Calculating Negative Powers

\[ 2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8} \] \[ 5^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2} = \frac{1}{25} \]

A Negative Exponent Does Not Mean a Negative Result

\[ 2^{-3}=\frac18 \]

The negative exponent changes the position of the power: the expression moves to the denominator.

Negative Bases

\[ (-2)^{-3} = \frac{1}{(-2)^3} = -\frac18 \]

Procedure

  1. Change the sign of the exponent to obtain a positive exponent.
  2. Place the power in its reciprocal position.
  3. Calculate the power.

3.5 Rules of Exponents

The properties of exponents allow expressions to be simplified without expanding every multiplication or division.

1. Product of Powers with the Same Base

\[ a^m\cdot a^n=a^{m+n} \]
\[ 2^3\cdot2^5=2^{3+5}=2^8 \]

2. Quotient of Powers with the Same Base

\[ \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n} \qquad a\neq0 \]
\[ \frac{5^6}{5^2}=5^{6-2}=5^4 \]

3. Power of a Power

\[ (a^m)^n=a^{mn} \]
\[ (3^2)^4=3^{2\cdot4}=3^8 \]

4. Power of a Product

\[ (ab)^n=a^n b^n \]
\[ (2x)^3=2^3x^3=8x^3 \]

5. Power of a Quotient

\[ \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n} \qquad b\neq0 \]
\[ \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{2^2}{3^2} = \frac49 \]

3.6 Variables, Coefficients, Exponents, and Constants

An algebraic expression consists of numbers, variables, and operations. Identifying its components allows the expression to be interpreted and manipulated correctly.

Main Components

  • Variable: a symbol representing a value that can change.
  • Coefficient: a number that multiplies a variable.
  • Exponent: a number indicating the power of a variable or expression.
  • Constant: a number that does not contain variables.
  • Term: a part of an expression separated from others by addition or subtraction signs.

Identifying Components

Consider the expression \( -4x^2+7x-3 \).

\[ -4x^2+7x-3 \]
  • Variable: \(x\)
  • Coefficients: \(-4\) and \(7\)
  • Exponents: \(2\) and \(1\)
  • Constant: \(-3\)
  • Terms: \(-4x^2\), \(7x\), and \(-3\)

Implied Coefficients

When a variable appears without a number written in front of it, its coefficient is \(1\). If it is preceded by a negative sign, its coefficient is \(-1\).

\[ x=1x \qquad -x=-1x \]
\[ 5x^2y^3 \]

The term contains the variables \(x\) and \(y\), with exponents \(2\) and \(3\), respectively, and coefficient \(5\).

3.7 Algebraic Expressions

Algebraic expressions can be simplified by combining like terms, using the distributive property, and applying the rules of operations.

Like Terms

Like terms have the same variables raised to the same exponents. Only like terms can be combined through addition or subtraction.

\[ 4x+7x=11x \] \[ 9x^2-4x^2=5x^2 \]

Distributive Property

The distributive property allows a factor to be multiplied by every term inside parentheses.

\[ 3(x+4)=3x+12 \] \[ 2(3x-5)=6x-10 \]

Simplifying Expressions

To simplify an expression, remove parentheses when appropriate, apply the necessary properties, and finally combine like terms.

\[ 3x+5+2x-1 \] \[ =(3x+2x)+(5-1) \] \[ =5x+4 \]

Evaluating Expressions

Evaluating an expression means replacing the variable with a given value and performing the operations according to the appropriate order.

\[ 3x^2+2x-1 \]

If \(x=2\):

\[ 3(2)^2+2(2)-1 = 12+4-1 = 15 \]

Using the Rules of Exponents

The laws of exponents are also used when simplifying algebraic expressions containing powers.

\[ x^3\cdot x^2=x^5 \] \[ (2x^2)^3=8x^6 \]

3.8 Applications in Radiology

Powers, variables, and algebraic expressions allow mathematical relationships used to describe different quantities in radiology to be represented.

Intensity and Distance

\[ I=\frac{k}{d^2} \]
  • \(I\) represents intensity.
  • \(k\) represents a constant in the model.
  • \(d\) represents distance.
  • The exponent \(2\) indicates that distance is squared.

Time and Current

\[ mAs=mA\cdot s \]
  • \(mA\) represents electric current.
  • \(s\) represents time.
  • \(mAs\) represents the product of both quantities.

Algebraic Models

A relationship between quantities can be expressed using variables, coefficients, constants, and operations.

\[ R=2x^2+3x \]

In this type of model, the expression can be evaluated for different values of \(x\) and simplified using the rules studied in the chapter.

Connection to the Chapter

These applications integrate exponential notation, order of operations, laws of exponents, and interpretation of algebraic expressions.

3.9 Application of Powers and Algebraic Expressions in Radiological Studies

In radiological studies, relationships between quantities can be represented using powers and algebraic expressions. Correctly interpreting these expressions requires applying the rules studied in this chapter.

Mathematical Model

\[ I=\frac{k}{d^2} \]

This model combines a constant, a variable, and a power. To work with it, each component must be identified and the order of operations must be followed.

Algebraic Interpretation

\[ 2x^2+3x-4 \]
  • \(2x^2\) is a term with coefficient \(2\), variable \(x\), and exponent \(2\).
  • \(3x\) is a linear term with coefficient \(3\).
  • \(-4\) is a constant.

Use of Exponents

Powers can represent quadratic relationships, scale factors, and other mathematical relationships within a model. Zero and negative exponents also allow factors equal to one or reciprocals to be expressed.

Integration of Concepts

Interpreting a radiological model may require identifying its algebraic components, applying the laws of exponents, following the order of operations, and evaluating the expression for given values.
