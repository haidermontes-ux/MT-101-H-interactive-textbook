1.1 Natural Numbers

Natural numbers are the numbers used for counting objects, people, or events. They represent one of the most basic numerical sets in mathematics and are fundamental for performing simple arithmetic operations.

The set of natural numbers is represented using the symbol \(\mathbb{N}\).

\[ \mathbb{N} = \{1,2,3,4,5,\dots\} \]

Natural numbers do not include negative numbers, fractions, or decimals. In many mathematical contexts, zero may also be included in the set of natural numbers depending on the definition being used.

🧠 Concept

Natural numbers are mainly used to count discrete quantities.

Example 1: The number of patients treated in a clinic can be represented using natural numbers. \[ 25 \text{ pacientes} \]
Example 2: The number of X-ray procedures completed during a hospital shift can also be represented using natural numbers. \[ 48 \text{ radiografías} \]

Within the set of natural numbers, there are important subsets such as even numbers, odd numbers, and prime numbers. These subsets are frequently used in mathematics, science, and technology to solve different types of problems.

Even numbers: These are natural numbers divisible by 2, meaning their remainder is zero when divided by 2. Examples: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10,... Even numbers are used to represent quantities that can be grouped equally.

Odd numbers: These are natural numbers that are not divisible by 2. Examples: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9,... Odd numbers are commonly used to represent quantities that cannot be divided exactly into two equal groups.

Prime numbers: These are natural numbers greater than 1 that have exactly two positive divisors: 1 and the number itself. Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11,... Prime numbers are fundamental in areas such as cryptography, computer security, and number theory.

🧬 Health Application

Prime numbers are important in mathematics and technology because they help develop security and information organization methods used in modern medical systems.

⚠ Common Mistake

Natural numbers do not include negative numbers or decimal values.

✍ Self‑Checking Practice Exercises

Select all values that can be modeled using natural numbers.

1.2 Integers

Integers include all natural numbers, their negative opposites, and zero. This numerical set allows the representation of both positive and negative quantities in mathematical and real-world situations.

The set of integers is represented using the symbol \(\mathbb{Z}\).

\[ \mathbb{Z} = \{\dots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\dots\} \]

Integers do not include fractions or decimal numbers.

🧠 Concept

Positive integers represent increases or quantities above zero, while negative integers represent decreases or quantities below zero.

Example 1: A temperature of \(-5^\circ C\) can be represented using a negative integer.
Example 2: An elevator located three floors below the main level may be represented by the integer \(-3\).
🩺 Health Application

In health sciences, integers may be used to represent temperature changes, pressure differences, gains or losses in intensity, and variations recorded by medical equipment.

Uses of Integers

  • Temperatures below zero.
  • Financial gains and losses.
  • Changes in elevation above and below sea level.
  • Relative levels or positions in medical equipment and hospital buildings.
⚠ Common Mistake

Decimal numbers and fractions do not belong to the set of integers.

  2. Write five negative integers.
  3. Explain why the number \(0\) belongs to the set of integers.
  4. Describe a real-life situation where a negative integer may be used.

1.3 Rational Numbers

Rational numbers are numbers that can be written as a fraction between two integers, where the denominator is different from zero.

The set of rational numbers is represented using the symbol \(\mathbb{Q}\).

\[ \mathbb{Q} = \left\{\frac{a}{b} : a,b \in \mathbb{Z}, b \neq 0 \right\} \]

Rational numbers include fractions, integers, and certain decimal numbers.

🧠 Concept

Every integer can also be written as a fraction, for example: \(5 = \frac{5}{1}\).

Example 1: The number \(\frac{3}{4}\) is rational because it can be written as a fraction between integers.
Example 2: The number \(-8\) is also rational because it can be written as \(\frac{-8}{1}\).

Finite Decimals

Some rational numbers have finite decimal representations.

\[ 0.25 = \frac{1}{4} \] \[ 2.5 = \frac{5}{2} \]

Repeating Decimals

Other rational numbers have repeating or periodic infinite decimals.

\[ 0.3333\dots = \frac{1}{3} \] \[ 0.727272\dots = \frac{72}{99} \]
🩺 Health Application

In medical imaging and radiology, rational numbers may be used to represent radiation doses, anatomical measurements, and exposure times with fractional or decimal values.

⚠ Common Mistake

Not all decimal numbers are irrational. Finite decimals and repeating decimals are rational.

✍ Practice Exercises

  1. Determine which of the following numbers are rational: \(\frac{2}{5}\), \(0.75\), \(-4\), \(\sqrt{2}\).
  2. Write the following numbers as fractions: \(0.5\), \(2.25\), \(3\).
  3. Indicate whether each decimal is finite or repeating: \(0.125\), \(0.7777\dots\), \(1.4\).

1.4 Irrational Numbers

Irrational numbers are numbers that cannot be expressed as an exact fraction between two integers.

Irrational numbers have infinite non-repeating decimal representations, meaning their decimals continue indefinitely without repeating a fixed pattern.

🧠 Concept

Irrational numbers cannot be written in the exact fractional form \(\frac{a}{b}\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are integers and \(b \neq 0\).

Example 1: \(\sqrt{2}\) is an irrational number because its decimal representation never ends or repeats. \[ \sqrt{2} = 1.414213562\dots \]
Example 2: \(\pi\) is an irrational number frequently used in geometry and medical sciences. \[ \pi = 3.141592653\dots \]

Square Roots and Irrational Numbers

An important property is that the square root of any prime number is irrational.

This occurs because prime numbers do not contain perfect square factors that allow the radical to simplify completely.

Examples: \[ \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{7}, \sqrt{11} \]

All of these square roots are irrational.

⚠ Common Mistake

Not all square roots are irrational. For example, \(\sqrt{9}=3\) because \(9\) is a perfect square.

Uses of Irrational Numbers

  • Calculating circumferences and areas using \(\pi\).
  • Geometry and trigonometry.
  • Scientific and medical measurements.
  • Calculations related to waves, ultrasounds, and diagnostic imaging.
🩺 Health Application

In medical imaging and ultrasound applications, irrational values such as \(\pi\) may be used to calculate areas, diameters, and geometric relationships within anatomical structures.

✍ Practice Exercises

  1. Determine whether the following numbers are rational or irrational: \(\sqrt{2}\), \(0.75\), \(\pi\), \(\sqrt{16}\).
  2. Explain why \(\sqrt{5}\) is irrational.
  3. Write three examples of irrational numbers.
  4. Indicate which of the following numbers has an infinite non-repeating decimal: \(0.3333\dots\), \(\sqrt{7}\), \(2.5\), \(\frac{1}{4}\).

1.5 Real Numbers and their Representation

🧠 Concept

Real numbers include all numbers that can be located on the number line: natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, and irrational numbers.

All real numbers can be represented as points on the number line. The position of each number depends on its value: positive numbers are located to the right of zero and negative numbers to the left.

🩺 Health Application

In radiological imaging and health sciences, measurements may involve natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, and irrational numbers depending on the clinical context and type of analysis performed.

Relationship Between Number Sets

Numerical sets follow an inclusion relationship. Natural numbers are contained within the integers, integers are part of the rational numbers, and together with irrational numbers, they form the set of real numbers.
